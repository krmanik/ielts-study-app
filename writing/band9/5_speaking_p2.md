# 5 Speaking Part 2

1 minute to prepare, speak 2 minutes. Template: **Intro → Background (when/where/who) → Details (cover every bullet) → Feelings → Why it matters / wrap-up**.
Trick: prepare ~8 flexible stories. One story fits many cue cards (your grandfather = person you admire / old person / advice / skill someone taught you).

## Part 2 template
Q: Say the 5-step Part 2 template with one opener for each step.
Tip: Use the 1-minute prep to write 5 keywords, one per step — not sentences.

- **Intro:** {I'd like to talk about} [X], {which/who} …
- **Background:** {This was back in} [year], {when I was} … · {I first met him when} …
- **Details:** {What stands out most is that} … · {The thing I remember most clearly is} …
- **Feelings:** {I have to say I was} [absolutely thrilled / quite nervous] … · {It made me realise that} …
- **Wrap-up:** {Looking back, it was} [a turning point] … · {That's why it means so much to me.}

## Person you admire
Q: Describe a person you admire. Say who this person is, how you know them, what they have done, and explain why you admire them.
Tip: Also fits: an old person you know · someone who gave you advice · a family member you are close to · someone who taught you a skill.

I'd like to talk about my grandfather, who is {without doubt the person I admire most}. He grew up in a small village with very little money, and he {had to leave school at fourteen} to support his family by working on a farm.

What stands out most about him is his {determination}. Even though he had no formal qualifications, he {taught himself accounting} in the evenings and eventually {set up his own small business} selling agricultural equipment. By the time he retired, he had paid for all four of his children to go to university — something {he had never had the chance to do himself}.

I spent a lot of time with him when I was a child, and the thing I remember most clearly is that he never complained. Whenever I {was going through a difficult time} at school, he would tell me stories about his early life and remind me that {setbacks are only temporary}.

I admire him because he proves that {background doesn't determine your future}. Whenever I feel like giving up — including while preparing for this exam, actually — I think of him. That's why he means so much to me.

## Place you'd like to visit

I'd like to talk about Iceland, which has been {at the top of my travel list} for years. I first {came across it} in a documentary about the Northern Lights, and the images {completely blew me away}.

If I went, I'd hire a car and {drive along the ring road} that circles the island. I'd love to see the glaciers, the black-sand beaches and the waterfalls, and of course I'd {stay up at night} hoping to see the aurora. I've also read that you can bathe in natural hot springs outdoors even when it's snowing, which sounds {like a once-in-a-lifetime experience}.

The main reason I want to go is that it's {so different from where I live}. My city is hot, crowded and noisy, whereas Iceland seems {wild, empty and peaceful}. I think spending time in a place like that would {give me a real sense of perspective}.

{The only thing holding me back is} the cost — it's one of the most expensive countries in the world — so I'm {saving up} and hoping to go in the next few years.

## Useful object / gadget

Tip: Also fits: something you bought recently · a gift you received · something you can't live without.

I'd like to talk about my noise-cancelling headphones, which have {made a huge difference to my daily life}. I bought them about two years ago, when I started commuting by metro, {after reading countless reviews online}.

I use them {pretty much every day}. On the train they {block out the noise} completely, so I can listen to podcasts — mostly English ones, as part of my IELTS preparation. At home, I wear them when I need to concentrate, because my street can be quite loud.

{To be honest, I was a bit reluctant to buy them at first,} because they weren't cheap. But I've definitely {got my money's worth}. The battery lasts for days, and they're comfortable enough to wear for hours.

The main reason they're so useful is that they give me {a bit of personal space in a crowded city}. Without them, my commute would be exhausting; with them, it's {become the most productive part of my day}.

## Book or film you enjoyed
Q: Describe a book you enjoyed reading. Say what it was, when you read it, what it was about, and explain why you enjoyed it.
Tip: Also fits: a film that made an impression · a story you heard · something you learned from the internet.

I'd like to talk about a book called *Sapiens* by Yuval Noah Harari, which I read {a couple of years ago} on a long train journey.

It's {a short history of humankind}, from the first humans in Africa to the modern world. What makes it special is that it doesn't just list events; it {asks big questions}, like why humans came to dominate the planet and whether we're actually happier than our ancestors.

{The thing I remember most clearly is} the idea that humans cooperate on a huge scale because we {believe in shared stories} — things like money, nations and companies, which only exist because we all agree they do. That {completely changed the way I think about} everyday life.

I enjoyed it because it was {thought-provoking but easy to read}. The writing is clear, with lots of humour and examples. I've {recommended it to several friends} since, and I'd love to re-read it in a few years to see whether I {still see things the same way}.

## Memorable journey
Q: Describe a journey you remember well. Say where you went, how you travelled, who you went with, and explain why you remember it.
Tip: Also fits: a time you got lost · a holiday you enjoyed · a time something went wrong · a time you were late.

I'd like to describe a train journey I took {from Mumbai to Goa} with three close friends about five years ago, just after we'd finished university.

We'd planned to fly, but {at the last minute} the flights became too expensive, so we booked an overnight train instead. {To be honest, I wasn't looking forward to it} — twelve hours in a crowded carriage didn't sound much fun.

But it {turned out to be the highlight of the trip}. The route goes along the coast through the Western Ghats, and because it was the monsoon season, everything was {incredibly green}, with waterfalls pouring down the hills right next to the track. We sat by the open door for hours, chatting and drinking tea from vendors on the platforms.

I remember it so well because it was {the last trip we all took together} before we moved to different cities for work. It taught me that {the journey can be more memorable than the destination}, and now I {deliberately choose trains} whenever I can.

## Time you helped someone
Q: Describe a time you helped someone. Say who you helped, how you helped them, why you helped them, and explain how you felt about it.
Tip: Also fits: a time you taught someone something · a good deed · a time you were proud of yourself.

I'd like to talk about a time I helped my neighbour, an elderly woman who lives alone, {learn to use a smartphone}. This was {during the pandemic}, when she couldn't leave home and was {feeling really cut off} from her family.

Her son had sent her a phone, but she {had no idea how to use it} and was too embarrassed to ask. When I found out, I offered to help. I went round every evening for about a week, and we started with {the absolute basics} — answering calls, then sending messages, and finally making video calls.

{It wasn't always easy.} She would forget steps, so I wrote simple instructions on cards with big letters. The breakthrough came when she {video-called her grandchildren} for the first time — she was so happy she actually cried.

{I felt incredibly proud,} not because it was difficult for me, but because such a small thing {made such a big difference} to her. It also {taught me a lot about patience}. We still chat on the stairs, and she now sends me jokes on WhatsApp!

I'd learn by {signing up for} adult classes at the sports centre near my home. I've heard the instructors are very patient with beginners, which is important because, {to be completely honest}, I'm a bit afraid of deep water after {a bad experience as a child}.

Secondly, swimming is {one of the best forms of exercise}, and it's easy on the joints. And finally, I'd love to be able to {enjoy beach holidays} instead of just sitting on the sand.

I think it would help me {overcome a long-standing fear}, and that kind of success often {gives you confidence in other areas of life} too.

## Website or app

{The downside is that it can be addictive} — the recommendations are so good that one video easily turns into ten. So I've started using it {more deliberately}, only watching things I've saved in a list.

## Festival or celebration
Q: Describe a festival or celebration that is important in your country. Say what it is, when it happens, what people do, and explain why it is important.
Tip: Also fits: a family event · a wedding you went to · a special day · a time you were happy.

I'd like to talk about Diwali, {the festival of lights}, which is {one of the most important festivals in India}. It usually takes place in October or November and {lasts for about five days}.

{In the run-up to the festival,} people clean and decorate their homes, and buy new clothes and sweets. On the main night, every house is {lit up with} small oil lamps and fairy lights, and families gather to pray, {exchange gifts} and share an enormous meal. Later, there are fireworks — although many cities have {cracked down on them} recently because of air pollution.

For me, the best part is {the sense of togetherness}. My relatives travel from different cities, so it's often the only time of year we're all {under one roof}.

It's important because it symbolises {the victory of light over darkness}, and of good over evil. Beyond the religious meaning, it's {a chance to reconnect with family} and {leave the past year behind}.

## Important decision

At the time, I was working in sales. It was {well paid}, but I {wasn't finding it fulfilling} at all, and I'd started to dread Monday mornings. I'd always been interested in computers, so I began {teaching myself programming} in the evenings.

{The decision wasn't easy.} I spent months {weighing up the pros and cons}. My parents were worried, since I'd be giving up a stable salary. In the end, I made a list of what I wanted in five years' time, and {it became obvious that} staying wasn't an option. I did a six-month course, and {it paid off} — I got my first developer job soon after.

It was important because it taught me that {it's never too late to change direction}. I earn a bit less than before, but I'm {far more motivated}, and it's actually the reason I'm taking IELTS — I'd like to work abroad in tech.

## Advice you received
Q: Describe a piece of good advice you received. Say who gave it, when you received it, what it was, and explain why it was useful.
Tip: Also fits: a person who influenced you · a teacher you remember · something you learned from an older person.

I'd like to talk about a piece of advice my first manager gave me: {"Done is better than perfect."}

This was {in my first year of work}. I was given a report to write and spent so long trying to make it perfect that {I missed the deadline}. Instead of getting angry, my manager {took me aside} and explained that a good piece of work delivered on time is {far more valuable than} a perfect one delivered late.

{At first, I found it hard to accept,} because I'd always been a perfectionist. But I started applying it — {setting myself time limits} and getting feedback early instead of polishing things for hours.

It was useful because it {reduced my stress enormously} and made me more productive. I use it even now with IELTS writing: {rather than aiming for a flawless essay}, I make sure I finish both tasks in the time. {It's simple, but it's changed the way I work.}
