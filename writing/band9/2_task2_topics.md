# 2 Task 2 Topic Bank

14 topics that cover most Task 2 questions (and Speaking Part 3).
Each topic: an Ideas card (common question + ready arguments with examples) and a Collocations card.
Ideas are reusable: the same argument works for many questions on that topic.

## Education — ideas
Q: Common question: "Should schools teach practical life skills (e.g. managing money) instead of academic subjects?" — give 2 ideas each side.
Tip: Also asked: online vs classroom learning · single-sex schools · should university be free · are exams the best way to assess students · teachers vs technology.

- FOR practical skills: many school-leavers {lack basic financial literacy}, e.g. they take on debts they do not understand.
- FOR: employers complain that graduates {are ill-equipped for the workplace}; teamwork and communication can be taught.
- FOR academic: core subjects {develop critical thinking}, which transfers to every area of life.
- FOR academic: practical skills are better {learnt at home or on the job}; schools have limited time.

## Education — collocations
- Many school-leavers choose to {pursue higher education} abroad.
- {Rote learning} produces students who can repeat facts but cannot apply them.
- Arts and sport are essential for {a well-rounded education}.
- Students {acquire knowledge and skills} that prepare them for employment.
- Children from poor families are more likely to {drop out of school}.
- In a fast-changing economy, {lifelong learning} is no longer optional.
- Parental involvement is strongly linked to {academic achievement}.
- Internships {bridge the gap between theory and practice}.

## Technology — ideas
Q: Common question: "Has technology made our lives more complicated or easier?" — give 2 ideas each side.
Tip: Also asked: children and screens · AI replacing jobs · online friendships · cashless society · does technology isolate people.

- EASIER: everyday tasks such as banking and shopping can be {done in a matter of minutes} on a phone.
- EASIER: video calls allow families to {stay in touch across continents}.
- HARDER: constant notifications {blur the boundary between work and leisure}.
- HARDER: people must protect themselves from scams and {data breaches}, which older users find confusing.

## Technology — collocations
- Hospitals now use {cutting-edge technology} to detect cancer early.
- We have {become increasingly reliant on} smartphones for navigation.
- Social media is {a double-edged sword}: it connects and divides.
- Children need {face-to-face interaction} to develop social skills.
- {Technological advances} have transformed the way we work.
- Doctors recommend limiting children's {screen time} to an hour a day.
- Automation may {render many routine jobs obsolete}.
- Some see facial recognition cameras as an {invasion of privacy}.

## Environment — ideas
Q: Common question: "Is climate change the responsibility of governments or individuals?" — give 2 ideas each side.
Tip: Also asked: plastic waste · should flying be taxed · protecting endangered species · renewable energy · fast fashion · is it too late to act.

- GOVERNMENTS: only states can {impose legally binding targets} on industry, e.g. emissions limits for factories.
- GOVERNMENTS: large projects like renewable grids {require enormous investment}.
- INDIVIDUALS: consumer choices {drive demand}; if people buy fewer cars and less meat, companies adapt.
- INDIVIDUALS: governments in democracies {follow public opinion}; citizens must push for change.

## Environment — collocations
- Cycling to work is a simple way to {reduce our carbon footprint}.
- Countries should switch to {renewable energy sources} such as wind and solar.
- Rising sea levels {pose a serious threat to} coastal cities.
- Deforestation destroys the {natural habitats} of countless species.
- Some scientists warn that pollution may cause {irreversible damage} to the oceans.
- Several countries have banned {single-use plastics} such as straws.
- Governments must {take drastic measures} to cut emissions.
- Overconsumption leads to {the depletion of natural resources}.

## Health — ideas
Q: Common question: "Should governments tax unhealthy food (e.g. sugary drinks) to fight obesity?" — give 2 ideas each side.
Tip: Also asked: prevention vs treatment · public vs private healthcare · sport in schools · why people are less active · free healthcare for all.

- FOR: taxes {change consumer behaviour}; the UK sugar tax led companies to cut sugar in drinks.
- FOR: the revenue can {fund public health campaigns} or school sport.
- AGAINST: such taxes {hit low-income families hardest}, as they spend more of their income on food.
- AGAINST: obesity has many causes; {education is more effective than punishment}.

## Health — collocations
- Office workers often lead {a sedentary lifestyle}.
- Diets high in sugar can {lead to obesity}.
- Since {prevention is better than cure}, funding should go to education.
- An ageing population {puts a strain on the healthcare system}.
- {Life expectancy} has risen thanks to medical advances.
- Regular exercise improves {mental well-being} as well as fitness.
- {Processed food} is cheap but often full of salt and fat.
- Campaigns can {raise awareness of} the dangers of smoking.

## Crime & punishment — ideas
Q: Common question: "Is prison the best way to reduce crime, or are education and rehabilitation better?" — give 2 ideas each side.
Tip: Also asked: why young people commit crime · longer sentences · community service · should police carry guns · CCTV everywhere.

- PRISON: it {protects the public} from dangerous offenders and acts as a deterrent.
- PRISON: victims need to see that {justice has been done}.
- REHABILITATION: prisons often act as {breeding grounds for crime}; inmates learn from other criminals.
- REHABILITATION: Norway's focus on education and job training gives it one of {the lowest reoffending rates} in the world.

## Crime & punishment — collocations
- Most young people who {commit a crime} come from troubled backgrounds.
- Heavy fines {act as a deterrent} to dangerous driving.
- Ex-prisoners without jobs are far more likely to reoffend.
- He {served a five-year sentence} for fraud.
- Minor offenders could do {community service} instead of going to prison.
- Poverty and lack of education are {the root causes of crime}.
- Training helps prisoners {reintegrate into society} after release.
- Most {law-abiding citizens} support stronger policing.

## Work — ideas
Q: Common question: "Is job satisfaction more important than a high salary?" — give 2 ideas each side.
Tip: Also asked: one job for life vs changing jobs · four-day week · gap year · work-life balance · should young people be paid less.

- SATISFACTION: people spend {a third of their lives at work}; misery at work affects health and family.
- SATISFACTION: motivated staff are more productive, so {it benefits employers too}.
- SALARY: for many, a high income is {a necessity rather than a luxury}, e.g. to pay rent or support parents.
- SALARY: money provides security and {the freedom to pursue interests} outside work.

## Work — collocations
- Many people value {job satisfaction} more than a large salary.
- A four-day week could improve {work-life balance}.
- Ambitious graduates want to {climb the career ladder} quickly.
- Contract workers have little {job security}.
- Graduates face {a highly competitive job market}.
- Employees who work long hours without breaks often {burn out}.
- Part-time jobs let students {gain hands-on experience}.
- {The unemployment rate} among young people remains stubbornly high.

## Government spending — ideas
Q: Common question: "Should governments spend money on the arts (museums, theatre) when there are more urgent needs?" — give 2 ideas each side.
Tip: Also asked: space exploration vs problems on Earth · public transport vs roads · foreign aid · should healthcare be free · funding sport.

- ARTS: culture {defines a nation's identity} and attracts tourists, who bring money.
- ARTS: art spending is {a tiny fraction of the national budget}; cutting it would not fix hospitals.
- URGENT NEEDS: while people lack basic healthcare, spending on galleries {is hard to justify}.
- URGENT NEEDS: the arts can be {funded by private sponsors} and ticket sales.

## Government spending — collocations
- The government should {allocate more funds to} rural schools.
- {Taxpayers' money} must be spent responsibly.
- Healthcare should be {a top priority} for any government.
- Cuts to {public services} hit the poorest hardest.
- Critics regard space programmes as {a waste of resources}.
- Investing in research is {in the national interest}.
- Large {infrastructure projects} create thousands of jobs.
- {The welfare state} protects people who lose their jobs.

## Globalisation & culture — ideas

Tip: Also asked: should everyone learn English · international tourism and culture · disappearing languages · global brands.

- YES: of about 7,000 languages, many are {on the verge of extinction}.
- NO: globalisation helps traditions {reach a global audience}, e.g. Korean music and Indian cuisine.
- NO: cultures have always {evolved through contact with others}; change is not loss.

## Globalisation & culture — collocations

- Global brands threaten {cultural diversity}.
- Festivals help communities {preserve traditions}.
- The internet has turned the world into {a global village}.
- {Multinational corporations} dominate many local markets.
- Some fear a single {homogenous culture} will replace local ones.
- Student exchanges encourage {cultural exchange} and tolerance.
- Family recipes are {passed down from generation to generation}.

## Cities & transport — ideas

- {Pedestrianised zones} make shopping more pleasant.
- {Air pollution} causes millions of early deaths each year.
