# 6 Speaking Part 3

Abstract questions about society. Formula: **Answer (opinion) → Reason → Example → Contrast / future** ("ARE+"). 30–45 seconds.
Talk about people in general, not only yourself. Topic Bank ideas (deck 2) work here too.

## Part 3 formula
Q: What is the Part 3 answer formula? Give one phrase for each step.
- **Answer:** {I'd say that, on the whole,} … · {I'm fairly convinced that} …
- **Reason:** {The main reason for this is that} … · {This is largely because} …
- **Example:** Take [my country], {for example.} … · {A good example of this is} …
- **Contrast / future:** {Having said that,} … · {I suspect that in the future} …

## Phrases: buying time & unknown topics
Q: You don't know much about the topic. Give 4 phrases that buy time naturally.
- {That's a tricky one to answer, but} …
- {I've never really considered that before, but I suppose} …
- {I'm not an expert on this, but my impression is that} …
- {Off the top of my head, I'd say} …

## Phrases: speculating & comparing
Q: Give 4 phrases for speculating about the future and 4 for comparing past and present.
- Future: {It seems likely that} … · {There's a good chance that} … · {I wouldn't be surprised if} … · {In all likelihood,} …
- Past vs now: {Back in my parents' day,} … · {These days, by contrast,} … · {Compared with a generation ago,} … · {Things have changed beyond recognition.}

## Phrases: balanced & conceding
Q: Give 4 phrases to show both sides / partly agree.
- {It's a double-edged sword.} …
- {There are pros and cons. On the plus side,} … {but on the downside,} …
- {I can see where people are coming from, but} …
- {To some extent, yes, but} …

## Education
Q: Do you think the role of teachers will change in the future because of technology?
{I'd say it's already changing.} {The main reason is that} information is now freely available online, so teachers {no longer need to be the main source of knowledge}. Instead, they're becoming more like coaches — helping students {make sense of information} and think critically. Take online courses, {for example}: they can explain a concept perfectly well, but they can't notice when a student is struggling or losing motivation. {So I suspect that} teachers will become more important, not less, just in a different way.

## Technology
Q: Has technology made people less social?
{It's a double-edged sword.} {On the one hand,} people spend hours on their phones {even when they're with friends}, which I think damages {the quality of face-to-face conversation}. {On the other hand,} technology helps people {stay connected} who would otherwise be isolated — elderly people talking to their grandchildren abroad, for instance. {So I'd say} it hasn't made us less social overall; it's changed {the way we socialise}.

## Environment
Q: Whose responsibility is it to protect the environment — individuals or governments?
{I'm fairly convinced that} governments have to take the lead, {simply because} individual actions can only go so far. I can recycle and avoid plastic, but {that's a drop in the ocean} compared with the emissions from a single power station. Governments can {pass laws}, {set targets} and invest in renewable energy. {Having said that,} individuals still matter, because politicians tend to {act on what voters care about}.

## Work
Q: Why do some people prefer to be self-employed?
{The main attraction is} independence. Self-employed people can {choose their own hours}, decide which projects to take on, and they {don't have to answer to a boss}. {A good example is} the huge growth in freelance designers and developers, who can work for clients all over the world from home. {The flip side, of course, is} the lack of {a steady income} — there's no sick pay or pension, which is why many people {find it too risky}.

## Cities
Q: Why do so many people move from the countryside to cities?
{It mainly comes down to} jobs and opportunities. Cities {offer far more employment options}, especially for educated young people, as well as better hospitals and universities. {In my country, for instance,} millions of people have moved to major cities over the last twenty years. {The problem is that} cities often {can't keep up with} the population growth, which leads to overcrowding, traffic and {sky-high rents}.

## Family
Q: How has family life changed compared with the past?
{Things have changed beyond recognition,} really. {Back in my grandparents' day,} three generations would often live {under one roof}, whereas {these days} most young couples live separately. Families are also smaller, and in many homes both parents work. {I think the biggest change is that} families spend less time together — {everyone's busy}, and meals in front of screens have replaced long conversations at the dinner table.

## Health
Q: Why do you think many people don't exercise enough?
{I'd put it down to} modern lifestyles. Most people {sit at a desk all day}, commute by car or bus, and by the evening they're {too exhausted to work out}. {On top of that,} gyms can be expensive, and in big cities there aren't always safe places to walk or run. {I think the solution is to} {build exercise into daily life} — cycling to work, taking the stairs — rather than relying on willpower.

## Media
Q: Do you think people trust the news less than in the past?
{Yes, I think there's been a definite decline.} {This is largely because} of social media, where {anyone can publish anything}, so people have become used to seeing false stories. {On top of that,} many news outlets are seen as {politically biased}. {The worrying thing is that} when people stop trusting reliable sources, they often {turn to even less reliable ones}.

## Tourism
Q: What are the negative effects of tourism on local people?
{The most obvious one is} rising costs. In popular destinations, landlords {rent out flats to tourists}, which {pushes up rents} for local families. Take Barcelona {for example} — many residents have had to {move out of the city centre}. There's also the issue of overcrowding: {it can be difficult to go about your daily life} when your street is full of tour groups.

## Shopping & consumerism
Q: Why do people buy things they don't need?
{I think advertising plays a huge role.} Companies are {extremely skilled at} making us feel that a new phone or new clothes will make us happier. Social media has {made this even worse}, because people are constantly {comparing themselves to others}. {Having said that,} I've noticed that more young people are {turning to second-hand shopping} and minimalism, {so the tide may be turning}.

## Sport
Q: Should sport be compulsory in schools?
{Yes, I strongly believe it should be.} {Childhood obesity is on the rise,} and for many children, school PE is {the only regular exercise they get}. Sport also teaches {valuable life skills} like teamwork and dealing with defeat. {That said,} schools should {offer a wide range of activities} — not everyone enjoys football, but they might love dance or yoga.

## Ageing population
Q: What problems might an ageing population cause?
{The biggest concern is} the cost. As the proportion of older people grows, there are {fewer working-age people} paying taxes to fund pensions and healthcare. {Japan is a good example} — it's {facing a serious labour shortage} and has had to rely more on robots and immigration. {I suspect that} many countries will have to {raise the retirement age} in the coming decades.

## Science
Q: Is it worth spending money on space exploration?
{To some extent, yes.} Space research has given us {technologies we take for granted}, like GPS and accurate weather forecasts. It also {inspires young people} to study science. {Having said that,} I can see why people feel uneasy {when there's so much poverty on Earth}. {Personally, I think} the best approach is for private companies to {take on more of the cost}, so that public money goes to more urgent problems.
