# 1 Task 2 Essays

Five skeletons (one per essay type) + one full band-9 essay each.
Learn the skeleton first, then the essay. {Braces} = chunk to memorise. [X] = fill in.
Time plan: 5 min plan · 30 min write · 5 min check. Aim 270–300 words.

## Skeleton: Opinion (agree / disagree)
Q: Give the 4-paragraph skeleton for "To what extent do you agree or disagree?"
Tip: Same opinion from intro to conclusion. Never "partly agree" unless you can explain exactly which part.

**Intro:** {It is often argued that} [paraphrase the statement]. {I agree with this view to a large extent, because} [reason 1] and [reason 2].
**Body 1:** {The main reason I support this view is that} [reason 1]. {This is because} [explain]. {For example,} [specific example]. {As a result,} [consequence].
**Body 2:** {A further reason is that} [reason 2]. [explain + example]. {Admittedly,} [other side], {but this does not outweigh} [your point].
**Conclusion:** {In conclusion, I firmly believe that} [restate opinion]. If [action], [benefit].

## Sample: Opinion — free university
Q: Some people believe that university education should be free for all students. To what extent do you agree or disagree?
Tip: Pattern: intro gives opinion + 2 reasons → each body = 1 reason → concession inside body 2.

It is often argued that tertiary education should be funded entirely by the state so that every student can attend university free of charge. While I accept that this would {widen access to higher education}, I disagree with making it free for everyone, as it would {place an unsustainable burden on public finances} and benefit those who need help least.

The main reason I oppose universal free tuition is the cost. Degrees are expensive to deliver, and if governments paid every student's fees, the money would have to come from higher taxes or cuts to services such as healthcare and primary schools. This is particularly unfair because many taxpayers never attend university themselves, so they would be {subsidising the future earnings of} a relatively privileged group. Graduates typically earn considerably more over their lifetimes than non-graduates, which makes it reasonable to expect them to contribute to the cost of their own education.

A further reason is that free tuition does not necessarily help the poorest students. {The real barrier for low-income families} is often living costs rather than fees. A more effective policy would be {means-tested grants and loans}, which target support at those who would otherwise be unable to study. {Admittedly,} free education sends a powerful message that learning is a right rather than a privilege, {but this symbolic benefit does not outweigh} the practical drawbacks.

In conclusion, I firmly believe that university should not be free for all. Instead, governments should {direct funding towards disadvantaged students}, ensuring that talent rather than wealth determines who receives a degree.

## Skeleton: Discuss both views
Q: Give the skeleton for "Discuss both views and give your own opinion."
Tip: Equal space to both views. Your opinion appears in the intro AND conclusion (and can open body 2).

**Intro:** {There is an ongoing debate as to whether} [topic]. {While some argue that} [view A], {others believe that} [view B]. {In my view,} [your side].
**Body 1:** {On the one hand, those who favour} [view A] {point out that} [reason + example].
**Body 2:** {On the other hand, I side with those who believe} [view B], {mainly because} [reason + example].
**Conclusion:** {In conclusion, although} [view A] {has some merit, I am convinced that} [view B].

## Sample: Discussion — early school start
Q: Some people think children should start formal education at a very early age, while others believe they should not begin school until they are older. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.

There is an ongoing debate as to whether children benefit from starting school when they are very young. While some argue that an early start {gives children a head start} academically, others believe that young children should spend their first years learning through play. In my view, the second approach is wiser.

On the one hand, those who favour early schooling point out that young children {absorb information remarkably quickly}. Starting reading and arithmetic at three or four, they argue, allows pupils to build a strong foundation before more demanding subjects begin. Early schooling can also be valuable for children from disadvantaged homes, who may not receive much intellectual stimulation outside the classroom.

On the other hand, I side with those who believe formal lessons should wait, mainly because {play is not a waste of time} for young children. Through unstructured play they develop language, creativity and the ability to cooperate, which are {the building blocks of later learning}. Finland, whose pupils do not begin formal schooling until the age of seven, {consistently performs well in international rankings}, suggesting that a late start does no lasting harm. In contrast, pushing toddlers to sit still and complete worksheets may {put them off learning} before they have had a chance to enjoy it.

In conclusion, although an early start has some merit, particularly for children who lack support at home, I am convinced that formal education should begin later. {The priority for young children should be} curiosity and social skills, not test results.

## Skeleton: Advantages outweigh disadvantages?
Q: Give the skeleton for "Do the advantages outweigh the disadvantages?"
Tip: The question asks for a verdict — say which side wins in the intro. Give the losing side 1 short paragraph.

**Intro:** [paraphrase]. {Although this trend has certain drawbacks, I believe the benefits are far more significant.}
**Body 1 (weaker side):** {Admittedly, there are some disadvantages to} [X]. {The most obvious is that} [drawback + example].
**Body 2 (stronger side):** {However, these drawbacks are outweighed by} [benefit 1]. {Moreover,} [benefit 2 + example].
**Conclusion:** {Overall, while} [X] {is not without its problems, its advantages clearly outweigh its disadvantages.}

## Sample: Adv/Dis — working from home
Q: More and more people now work from home instead of travelling to an office. Do the advantages of this development outweigh the disadvantages?

In recent years, a growing number of employees have begun working from home rather than commuting to an office. Although this trend has certain drawbacks, I believe the benefits are far more significant.

Admittedly, there are some disadvantages to remote working. The most obvious is that employees may feel isolated, as they {miss out on the informal conversations} that take place in a shared workplace. These interactions are not trivial: they help new staff learn from experienced colleagues and often spark new ideas. In addition, some people find it difficult to {draw a clear line between work and home life}, which can lead to longer working hours and stress.

However, these drawbacks are outweighed by the advantages. Firstly, working from home {eliminates the daily commute}, which in large cities can take two hours or more. This time can be spent with family, exercising or resting, which {improves employees' well-being} and, in turn, their productivity. Moreover, remote work {benefits society as a whole}. Fewer cars on the road means less congestion and lower carbon emissions, while companies can recruit talented people who live far from major cities. Many firms that adopted flexible working during the pandemic have found that output remained stable or even rose.

Overall, while working from home is not without its problems, {its advantages clearly outweigh its disadvantages}. The challenge for employers is to {provide opportunities for regular face-to-face contact}, so that staff enjoy flexibility without losing a sense of belonging.

## Skeleton: Causes / Problems + Solutions
Q: Give the skeleton for "What are the causes (problems) and what solutions can you suggest?"
Tip: Each solution must match a cause. Be specific: WHO should do WHAT.

**Intro:** [paraphrase]. {This essay will examine the main causes of this issue and suggest some practical solutions.}
**Body 1:** {The primary cause of} [problem] is [cause 1]. [explain + example]. {Another contributing factor is} [cause 2].
**Body 2:** {There are several measures that could be taken to address this.} {First and foremost, governments should} [solution 1]. {In addition,} [solution 2], {which would} [result].
**Conclusion:** {In summary,} [problem] {stems largely from} [causes]. {However, it could be tackled if} [solutions].

## Sample: Causes/Solutions — traffic congestion
Q: Traffic congestion is becoming a serious problem in many cities. What are the causes of this problem, and what solutions can you suggest?

Traffic jams have become a daily reality in cities across the world, wasting time and polluting the air. This essay will examine the main causes of this issue and suggest some practical solutions.

The primary cause of congestion is the {sheer number of private vehicles on the road}. As incomes rise in developing countries, owning a car has become both affordable and a symbol of status, and millions of new drivers join the roads each year. Another contributing factor is {inadequate public transport}. Where buses are slow and unreliable and train networks do not reach the suburbs, residents have little choice but to drive. Finally, poor urban planning means that many people live far from their workplaces, so {commuting distances have grown steadily}.

There are several measures that could be taken to address this. First and foremost, governments should {invest heavily in public transport}, creating fast, frequent and affordable services that are a genuine alternative to the car. Bus lanes and metro systems, such as those built in Bogotá and Seoul, have significantly reduced journey times. In addition, authorities could introduce {congestion charges}, which make drivers pay to enter the city centre at peak times; London's scheme cut traffic in the charging zone considerably. In the longer term, planners should {build mixed-use neighbourhoods} where homes, offices and shops are close together, reducing the need to travel at all.

In summary, congestion {stems largely from} rising car ownership and weak alternatives. However, it could be tackled if governments made public transport more attractive and discouraged unnecessary driving.

## Skeleton: Two-part question
Q: Give the skeleton for a two-part question (e.g. "Why is this? Is it positive or negative?")
Tip: Answer BOTH questions in the intro, one body paragraph each. Most common mistake: forgetting part 2.

**Intro:** [paraphrase]. {This is largely due to} [reason], {and I believe it is a} [positive/negative] {development overall}.
**Body 1 (question 1):** {There are several reasons for this trend. The most significant is that} [reason 1]. {Furthermore,} [reason 2 + example].
**Body 2 (question 2):** {In my view, this is a} [positive] {development because} [reason + example]. {That said,} [small reservation].
**Conclusion:** {To conclude,} [answer 1] and [answer 2].

## Sample: Two-part — living alone
Q: In many countries, more and more people are choosing to live alone. Why is this happening? Is it a positive or negative development?

In many parts of the world, single-person households are becoming increasingly common. This is largely due to economic and social change, and I believe it is a {broadly positive development}, although it carries some risks.

There are several reasons for this trend. The most significant is {greater financial independence}. As more people, particularly women, enter well-paid careers, they no longer need to share a home in order to afford one. Furthermore, social attitudes have shifted: people now marry later or not at all, and living alone is no longer seen as a sign of failure but as {a deliberate lifestyle choice}. Longer life expectancy also plays a part, since many elderly people continue to live independently after the death of a partner.

In my view, this is a positive development because it reflects {greater personal freedom}. Living alone allows people to organise their time as they wish, to focus on their careers and to develop self-reliance. It is also a sign that societies are wealthy enough to offer people genuine choices. That said, {the risk of loneliness} should not be ignored, especially among older people, whose health can suffer when they have little daily contact with others. Communities and local authorities therefore have {a role to play in} providing social activities and support.

To conclude, the rise in solo living is driven mainly by financial independence and changing attitudes, and on balance it is a welcome change, provided that those who live alone {do not become isolated}.
